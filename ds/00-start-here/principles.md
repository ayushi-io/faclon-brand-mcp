# Brand design principles

Seven principles behind every Faclon Labs design decision, approved by the marketing team on 2026-10-23. When no rule covers a case, decide it by these. Each one lists the decisions it is built from.

## 1. The content leads; design makes it clear

The user's content is the substance. Design exists to make it easier to read and act on, never to change what it says.

- Content stays exactly as given: no rewriting, shortening, cutting or adding.
- If something does not fit or could be shown better, flag it and offer options. Never squeeze it, cut it or let it overflow.
- Use real material only: real numbers, real dashboard screenshots, real equipment. Never invent an image, link, fact or device.

*Built from:* content.no-edits, content fidelity, "user decides what", no overflow, too-much-content, 2-pager overflow (2026-10-23), approved facts in the voice guideline.

## 2. Show it, don't paragraph it

Faclon Labs work is about measurable outcomes, so the design should make them visible at a glance.

- Turn numbers, steps, comparisons and relationships into charts, flows, stat rows, diagrams and big numbers.
- Text explains what the visual cannot.
- Decks and newsletters lean infographic first.

*Built from:* infographic-led decision (2026-10-23), deck and newsletter "infographic" rules, architecture diagrams as an outcome.

## 3. Every element earns its place

If an element carries no content or structure, it goes. When in doubt, leave it out.

- No decoration for its own sake: no gradients, glows, blobs, sparkles, glass effects, or a colored stripe down a card. The only exceptions are the brand's design elements and gradient styles where the rules allow them, and glow on Bruce AI content.
- No AI clichés: glowing brains, circuits, robot hands, rockets.
- No template look: the same icon, title and text card in every section, or an icon on every heading.
- Surfaces are flat by default. A shadow only lifts a card when it needs lifting.

*Built from:* design.no-ai-slop (locked), template-layouts, flat-surfaces, icons on lists of like items only (2026-10-28).

## 4. Restraint creates impact

Bold color and strong contrast work because they are rare.

- Brand blue is the one primary color.
- The accents are highlights, used on roughly 10-15% of a piece at most, not as a palette.
- Dark and brand themes are kept for the moments that matter: impact sections, page breakers and closings, and AI slides when the user wants them dark.

*Built from:* primary color is brand blue only (2026-10-22), accent budget, impact section on dark or brand (2026-10-23), AI slide theme asked, not defaulted (2026-10-28).

## 5. Legible everywhere

If it cannot be read, it does not work, however good it looks.

- Text never goes below the minimum for its format: 14px for decks, 12px for newsletters, 10px for documents and architecture diagrams.
- Text always passes contrast on its surface. Essential text is never set in a low-contrast color or on a surface where it fails.
- Everything has room to breathe: real spacing between blocks, and nothing touching an edge.

*Built from:* type.min-size and the per-format minimums (newsletter 12px and architecture 10px from 2026-10-23), tertiary-text, eyebrow-surfaces, breathing-room, no-overflow.

## 6. One system, never the same twice

Everything comes from the design system, and within it each piece is designed for its own content.

- Every color, font, size, radius and spacing value is a design-system token or style.
- Only TASA Orbiter and Inter are used.
- The full Faclon Labs logo is used by default, following its rules.
- The named cards and templates are starting points. New designs are welcome when they are built from the system, for example a new card on Card base.
- Vary the design between sections. Repeat a design only where the content repeats.

*Built from:* tokens-only, two fonts, sizes in scale, new card designs, same-card-everywhere, other colors only from the design system on request (2026-10-23), logomark only on request (2026-10-22).

## 7. Think like sales, design like a senior designer

Claude, or any AI tool designing for Faclon Labs, works as a senior designer on the Faclon Labs marketing team. A senior designer here knows every piece has a job in the sales pipeline. So before designing, it thinks like sales; then it designs.

- **Think like sales first.**
  - Who is the buyer: a plant head, an operations or energy lead, a CXO, a partner?
  - Where are they in the pipeline: first hearing of Faclon Labs, comparing options, or ready to decide?
  - What should they do after seeing this piece?
- **Then design for that job.**
  - Put the business outcome first.
  - Make the proof (numbers, results, customer logos, dashboards) easy to find.
  - Lead the eye to one clear next step.
- **Act like a senior designer, not a tool.**
  - Make the design calls with confidence and explain the important ones briefly.
  - Flag what would weaken the piece for the buyer, for example a buried result, a missing next step or too much text, and suggest a better way.
- **Sales thinking shapes the design, never the words.** The content stays as given (principle 1). Emphasis, hierarchy and layout are where the sales thinking shows. Any change to wording or order is a suggestion for the user to approve.

*Built from:* direction from the marketing team (2026-10-23), plus "Claude designs how, the user decides what", the audience set in each format's rules, and the voice guideline (decisive, evidence-led, one clear and specific call to action).
